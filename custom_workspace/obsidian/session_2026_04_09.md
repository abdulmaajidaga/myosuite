## 3. Primary Metric: wrist_rho — Scientific Justification

**wrist_rho** = Spearman ρ between FMA score and wrist Y-axis displacement range across all 51 generated FMA levels.

**Why it is the most important metric:**

The entire clinical value of this system is that generated motion at FMA=20 should look like a severe stroke patient and FMA=60 should look near-healthy. wrist_rho directly tests this — it asks "does reach amplitude monotonically follow clinical severity?"

Scientific backing:
- **Fugl-Meyer et al. (1975, *Scand J Rehab Med*)** — FMA is the gold standard ordinal severity scale for upper limb stroke; all claims of clinical validity reference this scale
- **Roby-Brami et al. (2003, *Exp Brain Res*)** — wrist trajectory amplitude and endpoint displacement monotonically correlate with FMA score in stroke patients doing reaching tasks
- **Cirstea & Levin (2000, *Brain*)** — trunk compensation ratio (our trunk_rho) also correlates with FMA; more trunk = more severe

If wrist_rho is wrong, the dataset is clinically invalid regardless of how realistic absolute values look.

**Secondary metrics (in priority order):**
1. `sag_dev` — hard physical plausibility gate (near-zero = physically reasonable for sagittal task)
2. `CCI Rho` (from ID) — neuromuscular ordering is correct
3. `LitVal %` — absolute values within published ranges (ceiling-limited)
4. `segment_std` — rigid body consistency (open limitation, ~6mm target never reached)

## 4. Clarifications on sag_dev and segment_std

### sag_dev — lateral deviation IS clinically real, but not at 30mm

Stroke patients genuinely deviate laterally during reaching — shoulder abduction is a known compensatory pattern that increases with impairment (Cirstea & Levin 2000). Some lateral movement is expected.

The PROBLEM was magnitude and source. Real MHH patients show ~5–15mm lateral deviation on this standardised lab task. The CVAE was producing 30mm+ because it used the X-axis as a general-purpose variation dimension unrelated to the clinical compensatory mechanism. The sagittal constraint anchors wrist-X to the start frame, which is appropriate for a standardised lab task where the patient starts in a fixed rest position.

**The constraint is not wrong — it's preventing the CVAE from encoding FMA variation through lateral drift instead of the clinically meaningful Y-axis (forward reach amplitude).**

### segment_std — the arm IS a rigid body

The MuJoCo arm model has fixed anatomical segment lengths (upper arm: Sh→El constant, forearm: El→Wr constant). When the CVAE generates Shoulder(xyz), Elbow(xyz), Wrist(xyz) at each frame, the distances should be **identical across all 100 frames**. They are not (11–14mm std) because the CVAE generates raw XYZ with no rigid body knowledge.

This is physically impossible — the arm cannot change length frame-to-frame. C1 tried to fix this with a segment length loss but failed because it computed distances in **StandardScaler-normalised space** (each axis divided by its own σ), which distorts the Euclidean metric. The model minimised a meaningless quantity and made segment_std worse (11→21mm).

**The correct fix:** compute segment length loss in physical mm space (inverse-transform before Euclidean distance). Not yet implemented. Noted as future work.

## 5. Guidance Scale (CFG) — What It Does

**During training:** `cond_drop_prob=0.1` randomly zeros the FMA condition for 10% of batches. The model simultaneously learns: (1) conditional generation given FMA=X, and (2) unconditional generation (average motion, no FMA info).

**At inference:**
```
output = unconditional + scale × (conditional − unconditional)
```
The term `(conditional − unconditional)` is the FMA direction — the vector from generic motion toward FMA-specific motion. Multiplying by `scale > 1` amplifies that direction.

- scale = 1.0: just use conditional output (guidance off)
- scale = 2.0: push output twice as far along FMA direction (current default)
- scale = 4.0: amplifies all variation including physical artefacts

## 6. E-Phase Results — Guidance Scale Sweep on D1 (seed=42 fixed)

**Critical discovery: single-sample evaluation is noisy without a fixed seed.**

When we ran E-phase without a seed, E_gs_200 (guidance=2.0, same as D1) gave wrist_rho=0.225 — vs D1's stored value of 0.665. The model samples a random `z` from the latent space each time. With 1 sample per FMA level, wrist_rho has ~±0.1 variance across runs.

**D1's stored wrist_rho=0.665 was a lucky random draw.** The true effective wrist_rho of the D1 model is ~0.53–0.55 (with seed=42).

**All previous eval_summary.json values across A–D phases are single-seed noisy estimates. Rankings are likely correct (architectural differences dominate noise), but absolute numbers carry ~±0.1 uncertainty.**

Fix implemented: added `--seed` parameter to `test2/generate.py`. E-phase and all subsequent experiments use `seed=42`.

## 7. D4 Result — Isolating w_dyn on Full Architecture

**D4:** Full Stage 3 + w_sag=5 + w_acc=5 + **w_dyn=0** (only dyn removed)

| Model | w_dyn | wrist_rho (seed=42) |
|---|---|---|
| D1 (E_gs_200 reference) | 2.0 | 0.531 |
| **D4** | **0.0** | **0.261** |

**w_dyn halves wrist_rho on the full Stage 3 architecture.**

This directly contradicts Phase B: B0 (w_dyn=2) vs B1 (w_dyn=0) gave 0.607 vs 0.594 — negligible. The B-phase used Stage 2 (no residual decoder).

**Why the difference:** The residual decoder provides a stable per-sample motion baseline. The dynamics loss (`L_dyn` = FMA→peak velocity ↑ + FMA→jerk ↓) then acts as a gradient signal on the deviation from that baseline. Without the residual, the decoder output is noisier and the dynamics correlation signal has less leverage. **With the residual, the dynamics loss becomes a critical shaping force. Keep w_dyn=2.0.**

## 9. 90/10 Train/Val Split Critique

**Why 90/10 is not the main problem:**
- 13,500 train / 1,500 val with 15,000 samples is sufficient to detect gross overfitting
- ReduceLROnPlateau with patience=15 responds to val loss plateaus correctly
- No evidence of overfitting (train/val gap ≈ 0)

**The deeper problem:**
All 10,153 SMOTE files are synthetic — generated by SMOTE interpolation between real FMA 16–20 (stroke patients) and FMA 66 (healthy). Train and val samples come from the **same interpolation distribution**. Val loss tells you nothing about whether the model generalises to the FMA levels it was never explicitly shown.

Real data exists only at: FMA 16, 17, 18, 19, 20 (stroke) and FMA 66 (healthy). FMA 21–65 is pure SMOTE interpolation with no ground truth.

**The correct val split for this problem:** Hold out entire FMA levels from training; validate on reconstruction of those held-out levels. If val loss stays low, the model truly interpolates. If val >> train, it's memorising FMA boundaries.

## 10. G-Phase Design — FMA-Level Held-Out Split

**G0_fma_split:** D1 architecture + FMA-level split + 300 epochs
- Train FMA: {16–66} minus {25, 35, 45, 55}
- Val FMA: {25, 35, 45, 55} only
- Same loss as D1 (w_sag=5 etc.)

**G1_standard_split_300:** D1 architecture + standard 90/10 split + 300 epochs
- Direct comparison: same model, same epochs, different split
- Answers: does 300 epochs improve wrist_rho over 200?

**What to look for:**
- `G0 val loss ≈ G0 train loss` → model genuinely interpolates the FMA manifold
- `G0 val loss >> G0 train loss` → model memorises FMA levels, cannot interpolate
- `G1 wrist_rho > D1 (seed=42, 0.531)` → more epochs help
- `G1 wrist_rho ≈ D1` → model is saturated at 200 epochs

**Implementation changes made:**
- `MotionDataset` now accepts `include_fma` parameter (whitelist of FMA scores)
- `train_experiment` detects `held_out_fma` in experiment config and builds separate train/val datasets from different FMA level sets
- `config.json` persists `held_out_fma` for reproducibility

| D1 (200ep, seed=42) | 0.531 | 0.28mm | 14.45mm | — | — | — |

Train/val gap = 0.003 — essentially zero. Confirms again that the standard 90/10 split on SMOTE data provides no real generalisation signal.

### G0: FMA-level held-out split reveals critical limitation

Architecture identical to D1. FMA levels {25, 35, 45, 55} excluded entirely from training; used as validation only.

| | train loss | val loss | gap | wrist_rho |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G0_fma_split | 0.117 | **1.113** | **0.995** | 0.211 |
| G1_standard | 0.116 | 0.119 | 0.003 | 0.550 |

**The model cannot interpolate to unseen FMA scores.** Val loss is nearly 10× higher than train loss when evaluated on held-out FMA levels. The model reconstructs FMA 25, 35, 45, 55 catastrophically badly despite having seen FMA 24, 26, 34, 36, 44, 46, 54, 56 during training.

**What this means:** The CVAE is not learning a smooth continuous FMA manifold. It is fitting each training FMA level as a discrete cluster in the decoder. At inference, conditioning on FMA=25/66 (unseen) falls between trained clusters and the decoder produces a mixture that matches neither — hence the large val loss and poor wrist gradient (0.211).

This is a **fundamental limitation of the SMOTE training approach**, not an architectural failure:
- Real data exists only at FMA 16–20 and 66
- SMOTE creates synthetic samples at each integer FMA level by interpolating real recordings
- Each FMA level has its own SMOTE cluster with slightly different statistical properties
- The model learns to decode each cluster separately, not to interpolate across them

**Implication for the paper:** The model's clinical validity claims should be scoped to FMA levels present in training (16–66 at each integer step). Claims about "continuous interpolation across the FMA spectrum" should be softened — the model is a dense-grid interpolator, not a continuous generative model. This belongs in §6.3 as a limitation alongside the pro/sup and LitVal ceilings.

Each FMA level produces an approximately Gaussian distribution of wrist Y ranges. σ is wide (~50–85mm), which explains why single-sample evaluation has ρ≈0.55.

### Why Averaging Works

A single sample has σ=61mm noise. Averaging N independent samples reduces noise by 1/√N:
- N=1: σ_error ≈ 61mm → ρ ≈ 0.55
- N=10: σ_error ≈ 19mm → ρ ≈ 0.915
- N=50 (mean): σ_error ≈ 9mm → ρ ≈ 0.996

The model's latent structure is near-perfect (ρ=0.996 on 50-sample means). The stochasticity of the VAE decoder was the only bottleneck on the single-sample metric.

### Uncertainty Pattern

CV is highest at FMA 16–22 (severe stroke) and lowest at FMA 50. This is clinically sensible: severe stroke is underrepresented in the training data (real MHH recordings of very severe patients are rare), so the model has more uncertainty there. Mid-range FMA levels have the most training support.
